- 2026-08-31 — **Empirical separation pre-check, run on the real 250×5 samples** (`src/separation_precheck.py`): 6 cheap "core" stylometric features (avg sentence length, Guiraud TTR, avg word length, first-person-pronoun rate, hedge-word rate, core punctuation rate) plus 4 "slang" features (emoji rate, exclaim rate, all-caps rate, meme-token rate), nearest-centroid classifier, 80/20 stratified split. **Key finding:** overall accuracy barely moves when slang/meme features are dropped (42.0% full → 41.6% core-only, vs. 20% chance for 5 classes) — separation is genuinely structural, not WSB-emoji-driven; this directly answers "is this just surface slang." **Second finding:** per-audience confusion tracks shared attributes, not noise — personalfinance↔povertyfinance (both conservative) and thetagang↔wallstreetbets/fatFIRE (each sharing one of thetagang's two attributes) are the dominant confusions, while audience pairs sharing zero attributes are almost never confused. This is real validation that the two-attribute (risk-orientation × sophistication) design is doing something, not just applying labels post hoc. No audience at/near chance — **no swap needed, design locked as-is.**
- 2026-08-31 — **Interpretation, on the record:** 42% vs. 20% chance is the *healthy* result here, not a weak one — these audiences deliberately share attributes and are expected to overlap; near-perfect separation from 6 cheap surface features would itself have been the red flag (a giveaway shortcut, not real signal). Moderate-but-structured separation, with confusion that tracks shared attributes rather than scattering randomly, is the expected and correct outcome for a genuine 2-attribute design.
- 2026-08-31 — Two limitations from this pre-check to carry into `METRIC.md`'s "what would a perfect score still miss": (1) these cheap features can't cleanly separate two audiences sharing a risk-orientation (personalfinance vs. povertyfinance) — a real blind spot in surface/lexical features, not a bug; (2) the `meme_token_rate` wordlist is crude and likely leaked generic finance vocabulary ("gain," "loss," "print") into the "slang" bucket, probably why fatFIRE's recall dropped most when slang features were removed — a feature-design weakness to flag, not evidence against fatFIRE's separation.
- 2026-08-31 — **Locked persona format.** Structured schema (not free text) — fields are the logged representation used for analysis; a fixed template renders them into one natural-language sentence per persona (matches how the guidelines' own worked example is structured-fields-as-prose underneath). Rationale: measurability — attribution (can ask whether a specific field moved the output), cross-audience comparability (same schema everywhere means within-audience variation numbers are actually comparable audience to audience), and reproducibility (no hand-written-register noise). One open-ended field included (a short concrete recent event/detail) so personas aren't purely enumerable. No field restates the audience's own defining trait (risk-orientation/sophistication) — that's carried by the audience description, not the persona, or generation would collapse into instruction-following rather than testing inference.
  - **N = 40-50 personas per audience**, fixed roster, generated once with a fixed seed and committed to the repo; baseline and improvement runs use the *exact same* roster (paired comparison — removes persona-to-persona variance from the baseline-vs-improved delta, so a real effect is easier to detect than with resampled personas per condition).
  - **Sampling:** most fields drawn independently (region, family status, the open-ended detail). One exception: age is drawn first, then occupation is drawn from a small age-bucketed lookup table (not full joint modeling, not vague "light guards") — the one field pairing most likely to produce a jarring implausible combination if left fully independent. Chosen over independent-sampling-with-ad-hoc-guards because an unspecified guard rule risks becoming an ad hoc decision made while coding rather than a committed procedure, and because the analytical payoff of full independence (single-field regression/ablation) isn't something Part 2 is actually scoped to do.
  - **Metric-design implication, carried forward:** at n=40-50 per audience, a classifier-with-train/test-split evaluation (like the separation pre-check used) would leave too few test examples per audience (~8-10 after an 80/20 split) to distinguish baseline from improved reliably — a single misclassification swings recall ~10-12 points. Decided to keep n at 40-50 (time/cost tradeoff, explicit) rather than generate more, and instead favor either a descriptive statistic (centroid distance / pairwise variance across all n, no split needed) or, if a classifier-style check is kept, cross-validation instead of a single train/test split. This is now a constraint on the formal metric choice in `METRIC.md`, not just a footnote.

Compliant, and the better design choice for signal quality.
  - **Named limitation (not a defect, a scope boundary):** we only test whether audience/persona voice-consistency holds across finance-adjacent situations, not across a genuinely unrelated topic. Carried to REPORT.md's "what we'd do with another week" — add a third, topic-neutral context as a stretch generalization test.

- 2026-08-31 — **Metric redesigned twice more before locking, both times catching a real flaw:** (1) candidate asked whether centroid-distance fidelity alone is enough — no: it only checks location (mean), not spread (variance), so a system that collapses within-audience diversity to near-identical texts could still score "perfect fidelity" if the average happened to land near the real centroid. Fixed by adding within-variation fidelity, using the real within-audience spread (from the 250 real posts/audience already pulled) as a reference, not just centroid location. (2) candidate flagged that "bigger between/within ratio = better" directly rewards that same collapse (shrinks "within" toward zero, inflates the ratio). Reframed the entire metric around **distance-to-real rather than maximizing separation** — an over-separated system is exactly as wrong as an under-separated one, since audiences were deliberately built to share attributes and partially overlap. Considered a coupled within+between score; decided **against** it — the task explicitly requires within/between reported separately specifically because a system can look strong on one while failing the other, and a coupled score is the one construction that would hide that. Finally normalized all three fidelity measures (centroid, within, between) as **signed relative error against each audience's own real reference** (centroid normalized by real within-spread specifically, turning drift into an effect-size-like number) — raw distances aren't comparable across audiences with different natural scales otherwise. Near-zero-denominator instability named as an explicit (currently low-risk) assumption in `METRIC.md` rather than left implicit.

## What we tried / what didn't work

- 2026-08-31 — Tried HF datasets-server `/filter` endpoint (SQL-like `where=subreddit='x'`) to query `winddude/reddit_finance_43_250k` server-side. Failed both unencoded and URL-encoded (`"Parameter 'where' contains errors or invalid symbols"`) — likely the dataset's parquet export isn't indexed for that endpoint yet. Worked around it by querying the remote parquet directly with `duckdb`'s `httpfs` (range requests, no full download) instead — ended up being just as fast and more flexible (full SQL, not a restricted filter grammar).
- 2026-08-31 — `USING SAMPLE n (reservoir, seed)` over the remote httpfs parquet silently returned ~9 rows instead of 250 per audience, with no error — even after fixing the syntax to `USING SAMPLE n ROWS (reservoir, seed)`, it still only returned 9/16,312 matching rows (confirmed via a separate `count(*)`). Root cause not fully diagnosed (suspect sample pushdown interacting badly with a selective WHERE filter over a streamed remote source), but rather than dig further, replaced it with `row_number() OVER (PARTITION BY subreddit ORDER BY hash(id || seed))` — deterministic, correct, and does one scan across all 5 audiences instead of five.
- 2026-08-31 — First "corrected" pull (the hash-ranking one above) silently included duplicates: dropped the `SELECT DISTINCT` from the original draft query without realizing why it was there. The source dataset is post/comment *pairs*, so the same post repeats once per top comment — without dedup, ~40-50% of each 250-row sample was actually the same ~110-120 posts repeated (worst case: a recurring "Daily r/thetagang Discussion Thread" post appeared 19 times). Caught by checking `unique(id)` counts per audience before trusting the sample. Fixed by deduping on `id` in a CTE before ranking.

## Claude Code: plausible-but-wrong moments

- 2026-08-31 — Proposed a 2×2 (risk-orientation × sophistication) design for the investing-forum audiences and labeled fatFIRE as "the intersectional audience." This was inconsistent: with a full 2×2, *every* cell (personalfinance, wallstreetbets, fatFIRE, thetagang) is equally defined by both attributes, so none is uniquely "the" compound one — the design erased the single-vs-compound contrast the requirement ("at least one audience must be an intersection... rather than a single attribute") implies. Candidate caught it by asking "isn't by your logic the others also an intersection in the same way?" Fix: made risk-orientation the single defining attribute for personalfinance/wallstreetbets/povertyfinance (sophistication left mixed/unspecified, matching the guidelines' own worked example), and reserved the deliberate two-attribute combination for fatFIRE (dev, intersectional) and thetagang (held-out, tests whether the compound pattern generalizes rather than being memorized from fatFIRE alone).
- 2026-09-01 — Proposed the Part 2 few-shot improvement by pulling real examples from `data/finance_forums/*.jsonl` into the prompt, without noticing those are the exact same files METRIC.md uses to compute the real-audience reference statistics (centroid, within-variation, between-separation) that the improved condition would be scored against. This is train-on-your-eval-set: the improved condition could score better purely by echoing text inside its own scoring reference, not by generalizing audience voice — a subtle contamination risk, not a design inconsistency like the fatFIRE case, but the same category of error the held-out-audience discipline exists to prevent, just at the data level. Candidate caught it by asking directly whether the few-shot examples and the evaluation reference were the same texts. Fixed by splitting each audience's 250 real posts into two disjoint, fixed-seed pools before any generation (`src/split_fewshot_eval.py`): an 8-post few-shot pool (prompt use only) and a 242-post eval pool (reference-statistic use only), verified disjoint programmatically. `METRIC.md` updated to reference the eval pool specifically, not the full file.
